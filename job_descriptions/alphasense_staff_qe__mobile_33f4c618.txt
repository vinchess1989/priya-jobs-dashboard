About The Role

Investors and analysts open our app between meetings, on a train, before a call, and act on what they see. Mobile releases go through app stores and cannot be rolled back, so a problem can stay in customers' hands for days.

Our mobile product is growing fast, with new AI workflows arriving on iPhone, iPad and Android at the pace of the rest of the platform. Our quality practice has real foundations, including Appium and native UI test suites and real-device runs, but our newest features are ahead of our automated coverage, and release confidence still depends too much on individual effort.

As Staff Quality Engineer, you own one question: can we ship mobile with confidence, every release, on all platforms? You decide what we test and what we deliberately leave out, and you turn what you build into a way of working that Mobile Engineering owns with you.

You will report to the head of Quality Engineering and work day to day with the iOS and Android teams, alongside backend, web and platform QE.

What Makes This a Staff Role


You set direction, not a task list. Nobody hands you a test plan. You look at the product, the release risk and the team, and decide what matters most and why.
You own the outcome across platforms. Your scope is the quality of the product across clients, not one app or one team.
You work through influence. You are an individual contributor. Your standards stick because engineers see their value, and success means they write more testable code, make better release calls and need you less over time.


What You’ll Do


Define mobile quality strategy. Set the long-term strategy built on "Quality by Design" and "Automation by Default": test layers, device and OS matrix, and release criteria for iOS and Android.
Set metrics the team can act on. Define quality metrics and coverage standards with Mobile Engineering and Product, so quality is measurable and sustainable without you.
Turn release day into a clear call. With no instant rollback, own the quality gates, staged rollouts and evidence behind every go or no-go decision.
Connect mobile to the rest of the platform. Mobile shares APIs and services with web, and customers update apps slowly, so changes elsewhere can break mobile after they have shipped. You are accountable for that risk.
Make the test platform dependable. Keep real-device and CI runs stable and fast (Bitrise, BrowserStack, GitLab, Allure), including test data and enterprise (Intune/MDM) builds.
Use AI where it earns its place. Use tools such as Claude Code, Cursor or Copilot yourself, and help the team decide where they help and where human judgment must lead.
Leave the team stronger. Coach engineers and QEs, review designs for testability, and leave behind standards other teams adopt.


What Success Looks Like


First 90 days: You have audited current coverage, agreed a prioritized plan with Mobile Engineering and delivered your first measurable improvement.
By 6 months: Critical mobile journeys are covered in the right layers on both platforms, and release readiness is an evidence-based call.
By 12 months: Mobile quality is a practice the team owns, and other portfolios borrow your ideas.


Who You Are


A quality engineer with 8+ years of experience, including 2+ at Staff or Lead level. You have connected quality strategy across web and mobile, not just worked on mobile alone, and set direction across several teams or apps as an individual contributor without people-management authority. You can show what changed because of it.
Strong hands-on experience testing native mobile apps on at least one platform (iOS or Android), with working knowledge of the other. You are comfortable with real-device cloud and CI/CD for mobile builds, can work with tools such as XCUITest, Espresso, Compose UI testing or Appium, and code in at least one language such as Kotlin, Java, Swift or Python.
Strong debugging skills across the mobile stack, from crash reports to network traces, and a good understanding of how mobile releases move through the app stores.
Excellent test design and API testing, and comfortable in the backend: you can read APIs, debug across the client and service boundary, and work with contracts.
Strong understanding of continuous delivery and release gating, with experience in test management systems such as Allure TestOps.
An active AI tool user, with examples of where it helped and where it fell short.
Practical about trade-offs, and able to explain a release call to engineers and to non-technical leaders.


Bonus Points


Enterprise mobile experience (MDM/MAM, Intune, SSO, mobile security standards such as OWASP MASVS).
Experience building a mobile quality practice from an early stage.
Mobile performance testing (app start time, memory, battery) and release monitoring (crash and performance signals, staged rollouts).
Experience across both iOS and Android.
